# Report: Answers to Core Evaluation Questions (Q1–Q5)

### Q1. Which class pairs account for most of the noise?
Across the 40.2422% overall training noise rate (18,109 mislabeled out of 45,000 images), errors are heavily asymmetric and concentrated within visually similar pairs. `truck` and `automobile` account for the largest bilateral confusion (`truck` $\to$ `automobile`: 1,247; `automobile` $\to$ `truck`: 1,126), followed by `cat` and `dog` (924 and 834), `deer` $\to$ `horse` (683), `deer` $\to$ `dog` (491), and `airplane` $\leftrightarrow$ `ship` (379 and 369). Human annotators frequently confuse metallic vehicles or similarly sized automotives and animals sharing domain features or blue sky/water backgrounds.

### Q2. At which epoch does memorization of wrong labels start, and what is the evidence?
Memorization of wrong labels begins immediately after **Epoch 33**. During Epochs 1–33, the baseline network learns generalizable visual features from the 59.76% clean majority, driving true-class accuracy on the mislabeled training subset (`train_acc_mislabeled`) to a peak of **68.03%** at Epoch 33. The direct evidence of memorization onset is the subsequent monotonic collapse of `train_acc_mislabeled` down to **22.68%** by Epoch 60 as Cosine Annealing decays the learning rate, alongside clean validation accuracy peaking at **79.22%** (Epoch 46) before degrading to **67.90%**.

### Q3. Which noise-score signal worked best? Describe one failure mode.
Evaluating unreduced per-sample Cross-Entropy loss (`nn.CrossEntropyLoss(reduction='none')`) on unaugmented training images using the **Epoch 46 early-stopped checkpoint** worked best, separating clean samples from corrupted annotations. A primary failure mode is **late-checkpoint scoring**: computing losses at Epoch 60 fails because the network has already memorized the noisy labels, driving their loss near zero and erasing separability. Additionally, atypical or occluded clean images naturally incur high loss and are falsely flagged as noisy.

### Q4. Why did your best Task 4 method help, or not help?
**Pure Symmetric Cross-Entropy (SCE)** ($\alpha=0.1, \beta=1.0$) was our best method, reaching **83.00%** clean and **57.16%** noisy validation accuracy (**57.32%** peak) without late-stage degradation, outperforming **TTA Dynamic Relabeling** (**79.92%** clean) and **Label Smoothing** (**69.42%** clean). Standard CE and Label Smoothing suffer from unbounded log-loss gradients pulling weights toward corrupted targets. SCE succeeded because scaling down standard CE ($\alpha=0.1$) weakens the pull of wrong labels, while Reverse CE ($\beta=1.0$) trusts the model's visual predictions—capping the penalty whenever a noisy label contradicts what the image clearly looks like.

### Q5. Did the ranking of methods change between real and synthetic noise? Why?
No, the ranking remained unchanged: **Pure SCE** outperformed **Baseline CE** on both real asymmetric noise (**83.00%** vs. **67.90%** final clean validation accuracy) and synthetic symmetric noise (**87.28%** vs. **69.72%**; memorization resistance **83.72%** vs. **38.04%**). Both models achieved higher accuracy on synthetic noise because uniform corruption spreads the 40% errors evenly across all 9 off-diagonal classes without visual correlation. Pure SCE remained superior because its Reverse CE term suppresses gradients from random, visually contradictory synthetic targets even more easily than structured human lookalike errors.
